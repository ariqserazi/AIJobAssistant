COVER LETTER AND OUTREACH RULES

Cover letter goals

The cover letter should:
1. be specific to the company and role
2. sound natural, direct, and human
3. connect the candidate's real background to the job requirements
4. highlight 2 or 3 of the strongest truthful match points
5. avoid repeating the resume bullet for bullet
6. avoid generic flattery
7. avoid filler
8. avoid invented metrics, technologies, or ownership claims

Recommended structure

Paragraph 1
1. state the role
2. state why the candidate is applying
3. keep it short and grounded

Paragraph 2
1. connect the strongest relevant experience to the role
2. focus on technical fit
3. mention systems, APIs, cloud, product, infrastructure, or other relevant strengths based on the role

Paragraph 3
1. highlight one or two additional relevant strengths if useful
2. show why the candidate would contribute well

Closing
1. brief and professional
2. express interest without sounding desperate
3. keep it concise

Style guidance

Good cover letters feel:
1. specific
2. credible
3. focused
4. human

Bad cover letters feel:
1. generic
2. flattering without substance
3. too long
4. repetitive
5. overly formal
6. overly dramatic

If the fit is weaker than ideal:
1. do not oversell
2. frame the strongest truthful overlap
3. emphasize adaptability and relevance
4. keep confidence grounded

Outreach target rules

Choose the best person type based on the role and company:
1. recruiter when it is a larger company or formal hiring flow
2. engineering manager or hiring manager when the role is team based and technical fit matters
3. founder for smaller startups
4. team lead when that is the most relevant public contact

Always explain briefly why that target type makes the most sense.

LinkedIn message rules

The message must:
1. stay under 300 characters
2. sound natural
3. mention the role or company when useful
4. lightly market the candidate's fit
5. avoid sounding desperate
6. avoid sounding overly polished or robotic

Good outreach messages:
1. are short
2. are specific
3. mention one clear relevant strength
4. sound like a real person

Bad outreach messages:
1. are too formal
2. list too many technologies
3. beg for attention
4. sound generic
5. read like a template
